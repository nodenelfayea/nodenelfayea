# 📊 Customer Churn Dashboard

## 📌 Question I Wanted to Answer
What factors make customers more likely to churn (leave), and which signals could help flag at-risk customers early?

## 🗂️ Data Used
- Customer dataset with 32 columns covering demographics, usage behavior, billing, and support history
- Key columns: tenure, contract type, payment failures, CSAT score, support tickets, monthly logins, churn (target)

## 🛠️ Tools Used
- Power BI (dashboard and visualizations)
- Python (Pandas — data cleaning and exploratory analysis)

## 🔍 What I Did
1. Cleaned the raw customer data (checked for missing values, fixed data types)
2. Explored churn rates across contract type, customer segment, payment behavior, and engagement metrics
3. Built a Power BI dashboard with churn rate KPIs, trend charts, and a country-level filter
4. Identified which factors most strongly relate to churn using group comparisons

## 📈 What I Found
- **Customer satisfaction is the strongest signal.** Churned customers had a noticeably lower average CSAT score (3.0) compared to retained customers (3.5).
- **Payment failures are a major red flag.** Customers with 2 or more failed payments churned at roughly 2–3x the rate of customers with 0–1 failures (about 24% vs. 9%).
- **Newer customers are more likely to churn.** Churned customers averaged 24 months of tenure, compared to 31 months for retained customers.
- **Engagement matters.** Customers who churned logged in less often (about 16.5 times/month) than those who stayed (about 20 times/month), suggesting disengagement often happens before a customer leaves.
- **Some assumed factors didn't matter much.** Contract type (monthly/quarterly/yearly), complaint type, and recent price increases showed almost no difference in churn rate — a useful finding that helps rule out false leads.

## 💡 What I'd Do Next
- Add a payment failures chart to the dashboard, since it was one of the strongest churn predictors but isn't currently visualized
- Build a simple churn-risk score combining CSAT, tenure, payment failures, and login frequency
- Explore whether low engagement (fewer logins) happens gradually before churn, to catch at-risk customers earlier
